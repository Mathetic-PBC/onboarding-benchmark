Detecting Frustration in Tutoring Dialogues: A Short Overview

By Ingrid Solberg · December 1, 2025

Frustration is among the most studied affective states in intelligent tutoring because it predicts disengagement. Detecting it reliably from a chat transcript is harder than it looks.

Signals

  • Text: negative sentiment, explicit giving up, sarcasm, repeated short messages
  • Timing: long pauses after errors, rapid repeated submissions
  • Interaction: help requests that escalate in specificity

Annotation

Agreement among annotators is moderate at best (kappa 0.5-0.7 in most studies). Distinguishing mild frustration from neutral terseness is the main source of disagreement.

Models

Lexicon methods miss sarcasm. Fine-tuned classifiers do better but need labeled data from the same population. Zero-shot language models are competitive and over-predict mild frustration in terse messages.

Ethics

Students rarely know their affect is being inferred. Using detection to change what a tutor does without disclosure is hard to justify; surfacing it to a human teacher is easier to defend.
