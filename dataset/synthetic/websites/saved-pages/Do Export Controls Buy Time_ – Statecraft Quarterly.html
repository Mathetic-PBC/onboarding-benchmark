Do Export Controls Buy Time?

By Elias Brandt · May 18, 2026

Technology export controls are sold as a way to buy time. The interesting question is what the time is for.

Chokepoints

Controls work best where production is concentrated in a few firms and countries: advanced lithography, certain chemicals, specialized design software. They work worst where knowledge diffuses through people and open publication.

The substitution problem

Every control creates an incentive to build a substitute. The question for policymakers is whether the time bought exceeds the time the substitute takes to mature. Historical cases suggest a range of five to fifteen years, with wide variance.

Allies

Controls require coordination among the few countries that hold the chokepoints. Each of them pays a commercial cost, and the coalition is only as strong as its least committed member.

Time is only useful if it is spent: on building capacity at home, on alliances, and on standards. Bought time that is not spent is a cost.
