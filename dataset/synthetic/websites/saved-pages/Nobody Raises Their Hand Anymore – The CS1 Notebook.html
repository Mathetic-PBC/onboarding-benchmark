Nobody Raises Their Hand Anymore

By Dana Whitfield · February 17, 2026

I have run intro programming labs for eleven years. This fall was the first time I walked a lab for an hour and nobody raised a hand.

The quiet lab

Every student had an assistant in the editor. The room was calmer, questions to TAs dropped by more than half, and our end-of-lab completion rate barely moved. The students who used to raise their hands were getting answers elsewhere. The students who never raised their hands were still stuck, now with nobody noticing.

Stuck looks different now

The old signal was a hand or a frustrated sigh. The new one is a student re-running the same failing code, or sending the assistant the same question five times with small changes. From across the room, that looks like work.

What I want from tooling

  • A view of who has been stuck on an error for more than a few minutes
  • Not the text of what students asked; that is theirs
  • A nudge for me to walk over, not an automated message to the student

Several research groups are building exactly this kind of instructor view from editor telemetry. I hope they keep the human in the loop. The best intervention I have is still a TA kneeling next to a desk and asking what the student thinks the error means.
