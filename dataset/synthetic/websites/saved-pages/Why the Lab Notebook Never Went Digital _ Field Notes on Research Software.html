Why the Lab Notebook Never Went Digital

By Tomasz Wierzbicki · June 30, 2026

Electronic lab notebooks have existed for thirty years. Most researchers outside regulated labs still do not use one. They keep a mix of paper, a running document per project, chat threads, and a downloads folder that doubles as a library.

What ELNs optimized for

ELNs were built for audit: timestamps, signatures, immutable entries. Those features matter for patents and clinical work. They do little for the person trying to remember why they changed the analysis plan in March.

Where thinking actually happens

In interviews with 31 graduate students, we found that the decisive reasoning for a project was most often recorded in a conversation: a chat with a labmate, an email to an advisor, and increasingly a long exchange with an AI assistant. None of these were linked to the notebook, the paper draft, or the data.

  • 27 of 31 kept a running document per project
  • 22 of 31 said their most useful notes were in chat tools
  • 4 of 31 could find the origin of a key decision within five minutes

What a notebook would need

A useful research notebook would import from the places where reasoning already happens, keep a link back to each source, and treat duplicates across tools as one item. It would not ask researchers to move their thinking into it.
