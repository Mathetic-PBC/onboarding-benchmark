Zone 2 Without the Hype

By Marta Lindgren · September 2, 2025

Zone 2 has become a wellness buzzword, which is a shame, because the underlying idea is old, simple and useful: most of an endurance athlete's training should be easy enough that you could hold a conversation.

Setting the ceiling

Lab tests define the zone by lactate. Most of us will never see a lab. Practical proxies work: a heart rate around 70-77% of a tested max, nasal breathing, or the talk test. Pick one and be consistent. The ceiling matters more than the floor.

Why it works

Easy volume builds the plumbing: capillaries, mitochondria, and the ability to use fat at moderate speeds. Those adaptations take months and years, not weeks, which is why base phases feel unrewarding.

The two common mistakes

  • Turning easy days into medium days because easy feels too slow
  • Abandoning the plan after three weeks because nothing seems to change

Measuring progress

Run the same flat 60-minute course at the same heart rate every month. If pace at a fixed heart rate improves, or heart-rate drift in the second half shrinks, the base is working. Expect both to move slowly.
