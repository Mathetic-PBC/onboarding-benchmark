@user

@assistant
The disagreement is a sign the current states conflate two things: *activity level* (idle) and *task state* (stuck). A student staring at an error is inactive but not off-task.

Proposed state: **Stalled-after-error**

**Definition**: The window starts within 120 s after a failed run, compile error, or failing test, and the student makes **no edits and no runs** for at least 20 of the 30 seconds, with the editor still focused (or the error/output panel open).

**Decision rules**
- If the student switches to a non-course tab (if you log focus) → **idle/off-task**, not stalled.
- If the student is editing the line the error points to, even slowly → **struggling**.
- If the student opens the help/hint panel or messages a TA → **help-seeking** (takes priority).
- If >120 s have passed since the error with no activity → **idle/off-task** (the error is no longer the likely cause).

**Boundary examples to put in the guide**
1. Error at 0:05, nothing until 0:30, editor focused → stalled-after-error.
2. Error at 0:05, scrolling through the file, no edits → stalled-after-error (scrolling isn't editing).
3. Error at 0:05, deletes and retypes the same line twice → struggling.
4. Error 3 min ago, no activity → idle.

The 120 s cutoff is arbitrary; check it against a sample before you lock it in.

### CONV reading-licklider
title: Licklider symbiosis vs Engelbart augmenting
start: 2026-02-01 22:10
projects: project_augmenting_cognition_reading_group
---
@user
reading group this week: licklider "man-computer symbiosis" (1960) and engelbart "augmenting human intellect" (1962). whats the actual difference between them, people treat them as the same thing. be concise
@assistant
They share the goal (computers helping people think) but differ in where the gain comes from.

**Licklider, "Man-Computer Symbiosis" (1960)**
- A **partnership**: human sets goals and judges; machine does the routine, clerical parts of thinking.
- Motivated by his own time study: most of his "thinking" time went to finding and preparing information.
- Focus on real-time interaction and the machine as a cooperating partner.
- Expected the partnership to be an interim stage before machines got smarter.

**Engelbart, "Augmenting Human Intellect" (1962)**
- **Augmentation of a system**: the human plus language, artifacts, methodology and training (H-LAM/T) as one system to improve.
- The gain comes as much from new **methods and conventions** as from the machine.
- "Bootstrapping": use the tools to improve the tools and the team using them.
- Explicitly about complex problems and collective work, not just individual speed.

In short: Licklider asks how the machine can share the work; Engelbart asks how to redesign the whole system of tools and practices so humans get more capable.
@user
which one is closer to what LLM assistants are today
@assistant
Mostly Licklider. Today's assistants are partners you converse with in real time that take over routine parts of a task, and the human sets goals and judges. That's his picture almost exactly.

Engelbart's part is mostly missing: few people change their *methods* around these tools in a deliberate way, and almost nothing bootstraps (the tools aren't improving the practice of the group using them). A discussion question for the group: what would an LLM tool look like if it were designed in Engelbart's sense, as part of a researcher's whole system of artifacts and habits, rather than as a chat partner?
@user
lol thats literally what we're trying to build with the notebook. ok good discussion q. also bush 1945 – where does memex fit
@assistant
Bush's "As We May Think" (1945) comes first and is about **memory and association**: the memex stores a researcher's records and lets them build and share "trails" of linked items. It's less about computation and more about retrieval by association.

So a rough lineage for the group:
- **Bush**: keep your records and the trails between them.
- **Licklider**: let a machine share the routine parts of thinking, in real time.
- **Engelbart**: redesign the system of tools, methods and training together, and bootstrap.

If your notebook keeps where each thought came from, that's Bush's trail idea most directly.

@assistant
A SAFE is an agreement where he gives you money now and gets shares later, when you raise a priced round. The two main terms decide **how many shares** he gets then:

- **Valuation cap**: the maximum valuation at which his money converts. If the cap is $8M and your priced round is at $16M, he converts as if the company were worth $8M, so he gets twice the shares per dollar the new investors get. The cap rewards him for coming in early.
- **Discount**: a percentage off the price new investors pay, e.g. 20%. If the round price is $1.00/share, he pays $0.80.

If a SAFE has both, he gets whichever gives him more shares. Many recent SAFEs are **post-money** cap only (the standard YC form), which makes the math clearer: his ownership at conversion ≈ investment / post-money cap. $50k on a $10M post-money cap ≈ 0.5%.

Things to know before Wednesday:
- What cap you and Hudson are aiming for, and why (don't let him set it by default).
- Whether others are in the round, since he'll ask.
- For actual terms, have a startup lawyer look before signing anything.
