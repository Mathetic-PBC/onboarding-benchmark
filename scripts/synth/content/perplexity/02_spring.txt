@user
what do people actually mean by "grand strategy", is it just foreign policy
@assistant
Not quite. **Grand strategy** usually means the relationship between a state's long-term ends and the full range of means it can use — military, economic, diplomatic, and sometimes cultural — under conditions of scarcity [1][2]. Foreign policy is the day-to-day conduct of relations with other states; grand strategy is the logic that (ideally) ties those choices together over decades.

Common threads across definitions [2][3]:
- **Ends vs means:** aspirations are unlimited, capabilities aren't, so grand strategy is about aligning the two.
- **Peace and war:** it covers how a state prepares in peacetime, not only how it fights.
- **Time horizon:** years to generations, which is why historians often describe it retrospectively (e.g., "Rome's grand strategy") rather than as a written plan.

Some scholars are skeptical that states really have coherent grand strategies at all, arguing they're mostly patterns imposed after the fact [3].

@user
if i chop a 2 second vocal from an old soul record and pitch it, do i legally need to clear it if i put the beat on youtube / sell leases
@assistant
In the US, **there's no safe length**: using a recognizable portion of a sound recording without permission can infringe even when it's very short, and pitching or chopping doesn't automatically make it non-infringing [1][2]. Two separate rights are involved:
- the **sound recording** (usually owned by the label), and
- the **composition** (publisher/songwriters) [1].

Courts have split on how small is too small — one well-known federal appeals decision held that *any* sampling of a sound recording requires a license, while another circuit later applied a "de minimis" test where tiny, unrecognizable uses can be allowed [2]. That uncertainty is why commercial releases clear everything.

Practically, for **YouTube**: Content ID may flag it, which usually means monetization goes to the rights holder or the video is blocked; a takedown is possible [3]. For **selling leases**: you're selling something you don't have rights to, which shifts risk onto your buyers and you. Options: use royalty-free/cleared sample packs, replay the melody yourself (still needs composition clearance for commercial release), or clear through a sample clearance service [3].

*Not legal advice — if a placement gets serious, talk to a music lawyer.*

### CONV student-telemetry-privacy
title: Privacy of student telemetry in research
start: 2026-03-13 23:40

---
@user
how do papers handle privacy for student keystroke/telemetry data. ferpa + irb, what do they usually write
@assistant
Typical practice in computing-education papers [1][2][3]:

- **IRB approval and consent.** Most studies run under an IRB protocol where data collection is part of normal course activity but *research use* requires opt-in consent; non-consenting students' data is excluded from analysis. Papers state the protocol was approved and how consent was collected.
- **FERPA.** Telemetry tied to identifiable students is part of the education record. Research use generally relies on consent or on de-identification; papers usually say data was de-identified before analysis and access restricted to the research team [1].
- **De-identification steps:** replacing student IDs with random codes, stripping names/emails from code comments and prompts, and removing free-text fields that could identify someone [2].
- **Instructor separation.** Many protocols ensure the instructor doesn't know who consented until grades are submitted, to avoid coercion [2].
- **Data release.** If datasets are released, keystroke timing is sometimes coarsened and prompts are scrubbed, because typing rhythms and writing style can be re-identifying [3].

Reviewers at HCI venues increasingly ask about **students' awareness of being recorded** and whether instructors could use the data for grading — address both explicitly.

[src] Re-identification risks in keystroke datasets | https://www.privacyinlearning.org/keystroke-reidentification | Typing dynamics as a biometric and implications for dataset release.

### CONV productive-failure-kapur
title: Productive failure and desirable difficulties
start: 2026-03-16 21:30

space: UIST related work
---
@user
productive failure (kapur) vs desirable difficulties (bjork) — summarize both and how theyd frame "when should a tutor intervene" in 1 paragraph each
@assistant
**Productive failure (Kapur).** Students are first asked to generate solutions to complex, novel problems *before* receiving instruction; they usually fail to find the canonical solution, but the generation phase activates prior knowledge and highlights critical features, so subsequent instruction is learned more deeply than with direct instruction first [1][2]. Framed for intervention: a tutor should *withhold* the solution during an initial exploration phase, as long as the student is generating and evaluating ideas, and intervene with consolidation afterwards. The key is that the failure is *productive* — the student is actively working, not stuck doing nothing.

**Desirable difficulties (Bjork).** Some conditions that make learning feel harder and slow initial performance — spacing, interleaving, retrieval practice, variation — improve long-term retention and transfer [3]. Framed for intervention: help that removes *all* difficulty (e.g., handing over code) can hurt learning even if it boosts immediate success; a tutor should preserve the difficulty that drives retrieval and reasoning but remove difficulties that are merely obstacles (e.g., environment errors, unclear specs).

Both frames support distinguishing **productive struggle from unproductive stalling**, which is exactly the line telemetry papers try to draw — Ostrowski et al. cite both as motivation [4].

[src] Productive failure in mathematical problem solving | https://www.learningsciencesreview.org/pf-math | Classic studies of generation-before-instruction.
[src] Desirable difficulties in learning — a primer | https://www.cogpsychprimer.org/desirable-difficulties | Spacing, interleaving, retrieval practice and their effects.
[src] Productive Struggle or Silent Stalling? (Ostrowski et al., L@S '25) | https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3698205.3729841 | Cites productive failure and desirable difficulties when separating struggle from stalling.

### CONV cohen-vs-fleiss-kappa
title: Cohen's vs Fleiss' kappa with three raters
start: 2026-03-25 23:55

@user
3 raters labeled all windows (5 categories). cohen or fleiss kappa? or average pairwise cohen
@assistant
With **3 raters who all labeled the same items** and nominal categories, the standard choices are [1][2]:

- **Fleiss' kappa** — designed for more than two raters; treats raters as interchangeable. Most common in papers like yours.
- **Average pairwise Cohen's kappa** (sometimes called Light's kappa) — compute Cohen's kappa for each of the 3 pairs and average. Useful if you also want to show pairwise agreement (e.g., one rater is an outlier).
- **Krippendorff's alpha** — handles missing labels and different measurement levels; good if not every rater labeled every window [2].

Recommendation: report **Fleiss' kappa** as the headline number and include the **three pairwise Cohen's kappas** in a footnote or appendix. If all raters labeled everything, Fleiss and averaged Cohen will usually be close [1].
[src] Agreement statistics for multiple raters — a practical guide | https://www.methodsforhci.org/multi-rater-agreement | Fleiss, Light's and Krippendorff's measures compared.
[src] Krippendorff's alpha explained | https://www.statsforannotation.org/krippendorff-alpha | When alpha is preferable and how to compute it.
@user +4m
we get 0.71 fleiss. how do i describe that, substantial?
@assistant
Under the commonly cited Landis & Koch benchmarks, **0.61–0.80 is "substantial" agreement**, so 0.71 can be described that way [1]. Many HCI papers phrase it as "substantial agreement (Fleiss' κ = 0.71)" [1][2]. Be aware that these benchmarks are conventions rather than statistical thresholds, and some reviewers prefer you also report per-category agreement so they can see which states were hard (e.g., stalling vs productive struggle) [2].
[src] Interpreting kappa: the Landis and Koch scale and its critics | https://www.statsforannotation.org/interpreting-kappa | Benchmark ranges and their limitations.

Report it as a small table: category, n windows, per-category κ.

@user
our class project labeled frustration on a 0-3 scale, 3 coders, some transcripts only labeled by 2. what agreement stat
@assistant
Use **Krippendorff's alpha with the ordinal distance metric** [1][2]. It fits your situation on both counts:
- **Missing data:** alpha handles items labeled by only 2 of 3 coders without dropping them.
- **Ordinal scale:** the ordinal metric treats a 1-vs-2 disagreement as smaller than 0-vs-3, which nominal kappa would treat as equally wrong.

Convention: α ≥ 0.80 is considered reliable, 0.667–0.80 acceptable for tentative conclusions [2]. For a 10-week class project, report the value, the number of units, and a brief note on where disagreements clustered. In Python, the `krippendorff` package takes a coders × units matrix with `nan` for missing labels and `level_of_measurement='ordinal'` [3].
